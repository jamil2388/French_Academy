# TCF Canada French Study Plan — 52 Weeks

**Starting point:** Absolute beginner (A0)  
**Target:** NCLC 7 in all four TCF Canada skills  
**Study time:** Up to 60 minutes per day  
**Suggested daily routine:** 10 min vocabulary review, 15 min structured learning/grammar, 20 min listening or reading, 15 min speaking or writing.

> This is a planning roadmap, not a guarantee of reaching NCLC 7 in one year. Progress depends on consistency, practice quality, feedback, and starting aptitude. Check the current official TCF Canada format and score requirements before booking.

## Phase 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–13)
**Goal:** Build an A1 foundation: pronunciation, essential vocabulary, basic sentence patterns, and simple everyday communication.

| Week | Learning objectives | Weekly practice / evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | French alphabet, letter names, accents, basic greetings, polite expressions, and key pronunciation contrasts. | Introduce yourself with your name and a greeting; practise reading simple words aloud. |
| 2 | Subject pronouns; *être* and *avoir* in the present; basic sentence order. | Write and say 10 short sentences about yourself. |
| 3 | Noun gender; definite and indefinite articles (*le, la, les, un, une, des*); plural forms. | Label 20 everyday objects with their articles. |
| 4 | Numbers, age, dates, days, months, and telling time; asking and answering basic questions. | Give your age, date, and a simple daily schedule aloud. |
| 5 | Regular *-er* verbs in the present; negation with *ne...pas*; common classroom and daily-life verbs. | Write 10 positive and negative sentences. |
| 6 | Family vocabulary; possessive adjectives (*mon, ma, mes*); describing people in simple terms. | Give a short introduction to your family. |
| 7 | Food, drinks, ordering politely, partitive articles (*du, de la, des*), and basic quantities. | Role-play ordering a simple meal. |
| 8 | Places in town, directions, transport, and common prepositions of place. | Ask for and explain simple directions. |
| 9 | *Aller*, *faire*, and common irregular present-tense verbs; *futur proche*. | Describe what you are going to do tomorrow. |
| 10 | Adjective agreement and placement; colours; describing objects, places, and routines. | Describe your home or neighbourhood in 6–8 sentences. |
| 11 | Reflexive verbs and daily routines; frequency expressions (*souvent, parfois, jamais*). | Describe a typical day in 1 minute. |
| 12 | Basic question forms (*est-ce que*, question words, intonation); listening for names, times, places, and numbers. | Complete beginner listening exercises and answer simple questions. |
| 13 | Review and consolidate A1 topics; identify gaps in pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary, and listening. | Complete an A1-level review test; record a 1-minute self-introduction. |

## Phase 2 — Everyday communication (Weeks 14–26)
**Goal:** Develop A2-level ability to discuss familiar situations, past events, plans, preferences, and practical needs.

| Week | Learning objectives | Weekly practice / evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Passé composé with *avoir*; past participles of common verbs. | Write 8–10 sentences about yesterday or last weekend. |
| 15 | Passé composé with *être*; agreement with the subject; common movement verbs. | Describe a recent trip or outing. |
| 16 | Contrast present and past; time markers (*hier, la semaine dernière, déjà, ensuite*). | Tell a short story in chronological order. |
| 17 | Imparfait: formation and common uses for descriptions and repeated past actions. | Describe what life or a typical day was like in the past. |
| 18 | Passé composé versus imparfait in simple narratives. | Write a short story using both tenses. |
| 19 | Object pronouns (*me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les*) in basic sentences. | Rewrite simple sentences using object pronouns. |
| 20 | Indirect object pronouns (*lui, leur*) and common verbs that use them. | Practise short dialogues about giving, telling, and helping. |
| 21 | Comparisons and superlatives; expressing preferences and making choices. | Compare two cities, activities, or products and explain your preference. |
| 22 | Future simple introduction; plans, predictions, and intentions. | Talk about plans for next year. |
| 23 | Shopping, services, health, travel, and making appointments; polite requests. | Role-play two everyday situations. |
| 24 | Connectors for sequence and reason (*d'abord, ensuite, parce que, donc, mais*). | Write an organised paragraph of 80–100 words. |
| 25 | Listening and reading strategies: identify the main idea, key details, speaker intention, and context. | Complete A2 listening and reading exercises; review mistakes. |
| 26 | Consolidate A2 grammar and vocabulary; review all four skills. | Complete an A2-level assessment; record a 2-minute talk about a familiar topic. |

## Phase 3 — Independent communication (Weeks 27–39)
**Goal:** Work towards B1: explain opinions, describe experiences, give reasons, compare options, and understand the main points of clear standard French.

| Week | Learning objectives | Weekly practice / evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | Review present, passé composé, imparfait, and future forms; choose tenses more accurately. | Write a 120-word account of an experience across past and present. |
| 28 | Conditional present for polite requests, advice, and hypothetical situations. | Give advice and explain what you would do in three situations. |
| 29 | Relative pronouns *qui, que, où*; combine short sentences into longer ones. | Write 10 sentences using relative pronouns. |
| 30 | More connectors: *cependant, pourtant, puisque, tandis que, en revanche*. | Write a paragraph presenting two sides of an issue. |
| 31 | Expressing and supporting opinions; giving reasons, examples, and conclusions. | Speak for 2 minutes on a familiar topic using a clear structure. |
| 32 | Subjunctive: recognise common expressions and learn a small set of frequent forms. | Practise sentences with *il faut que*, *je veux que*, and *bien que*. |
| 33 | Pronouns *y* and *en*; common combinations and word order. | Complete focused exercises and use each pronoun in original sentences. |
| 34 | Longer listening: conversations, interviews, announcements, and short news items. | Listen once for the main idea, again for details, then check a transcript. |
| 35 | Reading short articles and practical texts; infer meaning from context and identify argument structure. | Summarise a short text in 4–5 French sentences. |
| 36 | Writing organised messages and opinion paragraphs; paragraphing, register, and linking words. | Write 120–150 words and revise for clarity and accuracy. |
| 37 | Speaking fluency: describe, narrate, compare, justify, and respond to follow-up questions. | Record a 3-minute response; note pauses and recurring errors. |
| 38 | Listening and reading at B1 level; practise under light time limits and analyse incorrect answers. | Complete one focused listening set and one reading set. |
| 39 | Consolidate B1 skills and identify the weakest TCF component. | Complete a B1-level assessment or a mixed four-skill progress check. |

## Phase 4 — TCF Canada preparation (Weeks 40–52)
**Goal:** Become familiar with the exam tasks, improve accuracy and speed, and build performance towards the NCLC 7 thresholds in every skill.

| Week | Learning objectives | Weekly practice / evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | Learn the current TCF Canada structure, task types, timing, scoring, and official instructions. | Review official sample material; create a baseline score sheet for all four skills. |
| 41 | Listening strategy: understand context, speaker intention, key details, and increasingly challenging recordings. | Complete timed listening practice; keep an error log by question type. |
| 42 | Reading strategy: scan for details, identify main ideas, understand paraphrases, and manage time. | Complete timed reading sets; review why each wrong answer was tempting. |
| 43 | Speaking Task 1 preparation: introduce yourself and answer questions naturally with relevant detail. | Practise concise answers and follow-up questions; record and review. |
| 44 | Speaking Task 2 preparation: ask questions, gather information, and maintain an interaction. | Practise role-play scenarios; prepare useful question structures without memorising scripts. |
| 45 | Speaking Task 3 preparation: express and defend an opinion with reasons and examples. | Give structured 3–4-minute opinion responses and practise responding to counterpoints. |
| 46 | Writing Task 1: write a short practical message appropriate to the situation and audience. | Practise within the official task time and word limits; check task completion and clarity. |
| 47 | Writing Task 2: narrate, describe, or report information in an organised text. | Practise planning, paragraphing, tense consistency, and proofreading. |
| 48 | Writing Task 3: compare viewpoints and present a reasoned personal position. | Write a timed response with a clear introduction, arguments, examples, and conclusion. |
| 49 | Mixed exam practice: combine listening and reading under timed conditions. | Complete a timed practice session; classify errors as vocabulary, grammar, inference, or time management. |
| 50 | Mixed speaking and writing practice; improve recurring grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and organisation problems. | Complete one timed speaking set and one timed writing set; seek feedback if possible. |
| 51 | Full or near-full mock examination using reliable, current practice materials. | Record results for all four skills; compare each with the current NCLC 7 thresholds. |
| 52 | Final review and readiness decision; focus on the weakest component rather than learning many new topics. | Complete a final targeted mock or skill test; decide whether to book, continue preparing, or allow more time. |

## Daily study template (60 minutes)

Use this as a default. Adjust the activity to match the week's objectives.

- **10 minutes — Vocabulary:** Review Anki cards and useful example sentences.
- **15 minutes — Structured learning:** Follow the week's TV5MONDE lesson or study one grammar point.
- **20 minutes — Input practice:** Listening or reading at your current level.
- **15 minutes — Output practice:** Speak aloud, record yourself, write a short text, or revise previous work.

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | Main focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Grammar and sentence-building |
| Tuesday | Listening comprehension |
| Wednesday | Speaking practice |
| Thursday | Grammar and reading |
| Friday | Listening and vocabulary review |
| Saturday | Writing and speaking |
| Sunday | Review, self-test, and error log |

## Recommended resources

Use a small set of resources consistently:

1. **Duolingo** — daily supplementary vocabulary and basic practice: https://www.duolingo.com/
2. **TV5MONDE Apprendre** — structured lessons from beginner to advanced: https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/
3. **Mauril** — Canadian French listening and video content: https://mauril.ca/
4. **RFI Français facile** — listening practice and transcripts: https://francaisfacile.rfi.fr/
5. **Anki** — spaced-repetition vocabulary review: https://apps.ankiweb.net/
6. **France Éducation international — TCF Canada** — official exam information: https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada
7. **Official TCF sample tests** — familiarise yourself with the exam format: https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/exemples-epreuves-tcf

## Monthly progress review

At the end of each month, record:

- Listening: What can I understand without a transcript?
- Reading: Can I identify the main idea and supporting details?
- Speaking: How long can I speak clearly on a familiar topic?
- Writing: Can I produce an organised text with understandable grammar?
- Vocabulary: Which useful words and phrases can I actively use?
- Weakest area: What should receive extra practice next month?

## Important exam note

NCLC 7 is a score-based target, not simply a B1 label. Check the latest IRCC equivalency table for the exact TCF Canada score ranges, and aim to meet the required threshold separately in listening, reading, speaking, and writing. Do not book the exam based only on completing this calendar; use credible mock-test results and, ideally, feedback on speaking and writing to judge readiness.

**Consistency matters more than perfection:** if you miss a day, resume the plan rather than trying to cram two days into one.
